# Continuous Fruit Production, Transport and Distribution Process

The operation connects farms, packhouses, transport companies, empty-container depots, refrigerated storage depots, ports, shipping lines, client warehouses and retail outlets. Several activities happen at the same time: workers continue harvesting and packing while trucks collect cargo, containers wait for vessels, and clients receive earlier shipments.

## 1. Harvesting and movement from the farm
Farm workers pick fruit and place it into bins. Farm trucks collect the filled bins and transport them to the cold room or packing facility. Harvesting continues throughout the operation, keeping fruit moving from the orchards into storage and packing.

## 2. Washing, packing and cold storage
At the packhouse, workers wash, sort and pack the fruit into cartons. The cartons are arranged on pallets and held in the cold room until they are ready for dispatch. The packed fruit follows one of two routes:
- **Export:** pallets are loaded into containers for shipment through the port.
- **Local distribution:** pallets are loaded onto trucks for delivery to local markets, supermarkets or local cold rooms.

Forklifts move the pallets and load or unload them at the relevant facilities.

## 3. Transport planning and truck allocation
Transport company offices coordinate truck movements between the empty-container depot, allocated farms, packhouses, weighbridge, refrigerated holding depot and port. Different transport companies collect cargo from different farms. Export cargo is allocated to different vessels, so truck movements must follow the relevant loading arrangements and vessel schedules. Trucks may queue while waiting to collect an empty container, load fruit or discharge a loaded container.

## 4. Empty-container collection
At the empty-container depot serving the port, empty containers are washed and inspected before being made available for collection. A truck collects an allocated empty container and drives to the assigned farm or packhouse.

## 5. Loading and weighing
At the farm or packhouse, a forklift loads pallets of packed fruit into the container. Once loading is complete, the truck takes the loaded container to the weighbridge. It then follows the appropriate route according to the vessel schedule and whether the port is ready to receive the container.

## 6. Two routes for loaded export containers
**Route A: direct delivery to the port.** When the shipment can proceed, the truck travels to the port and joins the queue for entry and discharge. The container is removed from the truck and placed in the designated port stacks, where it waits to be loaded onto its allocated vessel. After discharge, the truck becomes available for another collection and may return to the empty-container depot to begin the next trip.

**Route B: temporary storage at a refrigerated depot.** If a vessel is delayed or the container needs to wait before delivery to the port stacks, the truck takes it to a refrigerated holding depot. The container is offloaded and plugged into an electrical supply to maintain refrigeration. The truck leaves and becomes available for other work. On a later day, a truck returns to collect the container and delivers it to the port when the shipment can proceed.

## 7. Vessel arrival, loading and departure
Vessels arrive and may wait for their turn to berth and load. Containers in the port stacks are loaded onto their allocated vessels. Once loading is complete, the vessel departs for the destination port. Meanwhile, other vessels arrive, trucks continue delivering containers, and farms continue packing new cargo.

## 8. Client collection at the destination port
At the destination port, the containers are discharged from the vessel and undergo customs inspection and clearance. The client arranges or rents trucks to collect the released containers and transport them to the client's warehouse. At the warehouse, forklifts remove the pallets from the containers and place the fruit into the appropriate storage area.

## 9. Distribution to shops and return of empty containers
The client distributes the fruit from the warehouse to shopping centres, supermarkets and other retail outlets. After unloading, the empty containers are returned to the shipping line's designated return location, completing that container's delivery cycle.

## 10. Local-market distribution
Some trucks leaving the packhouse follow the local route instead of travelling to the port. They deliver packed fruit to local markets, supermarkets or local cold rooms. The cargo is discharged at the receiving facility, and the trucks become available for their next collection.

## Overall operating flow
- **Export route:** Farm → fruit bins → cold room and packhouse → cartons and pallets → container loading → weighbridge → port stacks → vessel → destination port and customs → client warehouse → retail outlets.
- **Export route with a delay:** Container loading → weighbridge → refrigerated holding depot → plugged storage → later collection → port stacks → vessel.
- **Empty-container cycle:** Empty depot → washing and inspection → truck collection → farm or packhouse → loading and delivery → truck returns for another collection.
- **Local route:** Farm → packhouse → cold room → truck loading → local market, supermarket or local cold room.

The operation should be designed as a continuous network with separate loading, unloading, queuing, storage and container-return areas. Its layout must allow several trucks, containers and shipments to move through different stages simultaneously, including temporary storage when vessels are delayed.
